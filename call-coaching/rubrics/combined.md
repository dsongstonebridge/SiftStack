# Combined Call Rubric: Cold Call That Rolls Into Lead Management (Tulsa Homebuyers)

## Why this rubric exists

Added 2026-10-26. Until a cold caller is hired, Diego is both the cold caller
and the lead manager. When he has a seller on the phone he usually keeps going
and runs the lead-manager conversation right there, because booking a callback
with himself would be strange. Neither single rubric grades that call fairly:

- The **cold call** rubric spends 20% on booking a callback with a partner
  (5.4 scores 0 for "no booking attempt"), so staying on the line, which is
  the better move here, would be marked down.
- The **lead management** rubric opens with a continuity criterion (1.1,
  "reference the earlier conversation"). There was no earlier conversation,
  so the opener would score 0 and the cold-call skills would never be graded.

So a combined call is graded in three parts: the **opening** on the cold-call
criteria, the **switch** on new criteria, and **everything after** on the
lead-management criteria. One total, plus a score for each part, so the
opening stays comparable with a future cold caller's calls and the
lead-management part stays comparable with scheduled follow-ups.

## When to use it

A call is combined when it is a FIRST touch with the owner (no sign of an
earlier conversation) AND, on the same call, the caller goes past the cold
call's job into lead-manager qualification: digging into all four pillars,
roadblocks, condition in detail, a next step toward an offer.

The user can set the type when naming a call for transcription
(`--call-id CA...:combined`); that always wins. Otherwise the sorting step
decides. A first-touch call that only captures early motivation and books a
callback is a plain **cold call**. A call that reaches an offer number or a
contract is **closing**. There is no short version: a seller who declines in
the first 30 seconds was never switched, so that call is a cold-call short
report.

## How to grade it

1. Read this file AND `cold_call.md` AND `lead_management.md` in full. The
   criteria below point to those files by number; use their "What Great Looks
   Like", "Common Failure" and 0/3/5 anchors exactly as written there.
2. Find the switch: the first line where the caller moves from the cold-call
   job into lead-manager qualification. The sorting step suggests one in the
   transcript header ("switch at"); use it unless the transcript clearly shows
   otherwise, and say so in the report if you move it.
3. Part A criteria are judged on the call up to the switch, Part C criteria
   on the call after it. Category 6 (rapport, tonality, control) covers the
   whole call. Category 3 (the switch) is judged on the switch itself.
4. Auto-fails: any auto-fail condition in EITHER source rubric fails the call.

## Categories and criteria

Weights reflect the job of a combined call: open well enough to earn the
conversation (25%), switch cleanly (10%), then run a full lead-manager
qualification (65%).

### Part B: The Switch (new criteria, Tulsa Homebuyers)

**Category 3: The Switch (10%)**. Always scored on a combined call (never N/A).

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 Earned the switch | Switches only after the seller has shown openness: early motivation surfaced, or an answer that invites more ("we've thought about it", "depends on the price"). A seller who said they are busy, or a clear no, gets the cold-call move instead (callback booked, or the no handled), not a longer call. | Pushing a 10-minute conversation onto a seller who just said they are busy or not interested, or switching on a one-word answer with no sign of interest. | 0 = forced a deeper conversation on a seller who signalled busy or not interested; 3 = switched on thin engagement without checking; 5 = switched on clear openness |
| 3.2 Permission to keep going | Asks before going deeper: "Do you have about 10 minutes right now? I'd love to learn a little more about the place." Gets a yes before the deeper questions start. | Rolling straight from "any plans for it?" into condition, roadblocks and price with no check that now is a good time. | 0 = went deep with no ask; 3 = asked vaguely, or only after the deeper questions had already started; 5 = clear ask before the deeper questions, seller agreed |
| 3.3 Time, agenda and result set | Sets the rest of the call the way lead_management.md 1.2 sets a follow-up: how long, what it covers, what it decides, and an exit. "It should only take about 10 minutes. I'll ask about the house and your plans, and by the end we'll both know if it makes sense to put an offer together. If it's not a fit, that's totally fine." | Questions with no stated purpose, so the seller does not know why the call got longer or when it ends. | 0 = none of time, agenda, result, exit; 3 = one or two of them; 5 = time, agenda and result plus the permission to say no |

## N/A rules

- Part A (categories 1 and 2): any criterion may be N/A when the seller made
  it unreachable or the transcript lacks the signal, as in `cold_call.md`
  (example: the seller never asked how we got their info, so 2.1 is N/A).
- Part B (category 3): never N/A. A combined call switched, so the switch is
  always graded.
- Part C (categories 4 to 7): only 7.4 may be N/A (lead not yet qualified),
  as in `lead_management.md`.
- Every N/A needs a reason in `na_reasons`. The total redistributes N/A weight
  the same way as the other rubrics.

## Grade Bands

Same as the other rubrics: 90+ Elite, 75+ Strong, 60+ Developing, 40+ Needs
Work, below 40 Retrain; any auto-fail makes the band FAIL.

## Perfect Call Definition

A 100 combined call opens like a perfect cold call: the seller's name and the
exact street in the first two sentences, the decision-maker confirmed before
anything else, the awkwardness named, and the soft "any plans for it?"
question, then quiet. Any "how did you get my number?" is met with owned blame
and the list is never named. When the seller shows real openness, the caller
asks for the time rather than taking it ("Do you have about 10 minutes right
now?"), then sets how long, what it covers, what it decides, and that a no is
fine. From there it is a full lead-manager qualification: motivation peeled
past the first answer, timeline bucketed, condition graded and toured, the
pocket number pulled and reflected without negotiating, every roadblock named,
with nothing the seller already said asked twice. The seller talks more than
the caller the whole way through. It ends with the binary outcome honored: a
booked offer call with Jeff carrying the complete handoff package, or a
specific follow-up with time, agenda and result, at a cadence that matches
what the pillars proved.
